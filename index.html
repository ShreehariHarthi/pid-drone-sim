Applying Newton’s Second Law along the vertical axis (z positive upward), the vertical acceleration a_z of the quadcopter is determined by the net vertical force acting on its mass m = 1.50 kg:

m · a_z = T_total − m·g − c·v_z + F_wind
where T_total ∈ [0, 30.00 N], m·g = 14.72 N, air drag c = 0.15 N·s/m, and F_wind is vertical gust force (N).
T_total > 14.72 N

Net upward force (F_net > 0): drone accelerates upward and climbs.

T_total = 14.72 N

Force equilibrium (F_net = 0): drone maintains steady hover (a_z = 0).

T_total < 14.72 N

Net downward force (F_net < 0): gravity overcomes thrust and drone descends.

Module 2 of 3

PID Thrust Equation & Analytical Gain Calculation (K_p, K_i, K_d)

Given a desired target altitude z_set and measured altitude z(t), the altitude tracking error is e(t) = z_set − z(t). The PID controller computes the vertical thrust command (in Newtons):

T_PID(t) = K_p · e(t) + K_i · ∫e(t)dt + K_d · (de/dt) (Using derivative-on-measurement to prevent setpoint kick: de/dt = −v_z during constant setpoint)

Substituting a PD controller into Newton’s 2nd Law (m·z″ + K_d·z′ + K_p·z = K_p·z_set) yields the standard second-order closed-loop characteristic equation:

Analytical Gain Formulas
s² + (K_d / m)·s + (K_p / m) = s² + 2ζω_n·s + ω_n²
  • K_p = m · ω_n² — Sets response speed via natural frequency ω_n (rad/s).
  • K_d = 2 · ζ · ω_n · m = 2ζ√(m · K_p) — Sets damping ratio ζ (ζ = 1.0 gives critical damping with zero overshoot).
  • K_i < (K_p · K_d) / m — Per Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion. K_i builds I_term → 14.72 N to cancel gravity droop when e = 0, without causing integral windup.
Worked Numerical Example (m = 1.50 kg)

Suppose we want a crisp, critically damped response (ζ = 1.0) with natural frequency ω_n = 1.40 rad/s for a 50 m takeoff or 10 m step-down:

1. K_p = 1.50 × (1.40)² = 2.94 N/m
2. K_d = 2 × 1.0 × 1.40 × 1.50 = 4.20 N·s/m
3. Routh-Hurwitz Limit: K_i < (2.94 × 4.20) / 1.50 = 8.23
4. Practical K_i (to hold 14.72 N): 0.80 – 1.40 N/(m·s)

Note: In the simulator, sliders start at 0.00. Use these formulas to calculate your starting gains instead of guessing blindly.

Module 3 of 3

Why Only Vertical Lift (1-DOF) Is Simulated vs. Real 6-DOF Quadcopter Flight

A real physical quadcopter moves in 6 Degrees of Freedom (6-DOF): three translational axes (X, Y, Z) and three rotational angles (Roll φ, Pitch θ, Yaw ψ).

Why pure vertical lift is impossible alone in reality: If you run only an altitude PID controller on a real drone without attitude stabilization, any minor propeller imbalance or air disturbance tilts the drone. Because vertical lift drops as T_z = T · cos(φ) · cos(θ) and horizontal thrust accelerates the craft sideways, an unstabilized quadcopter flips upside down and crashes in under 0.2 seconds.

Cascaded Control Architecture: Real flight controllers solve this with two nested loops. A fast Inner Attitude Loop (400–1000 Hz) continuously drives Roll & Pitch to zero (φ ≈ 0, θ ≈ 0) while GPS/Optical Flow holds X, Y. Only because this inner loop holds the airframe level can the slower Outer Altitude Loop (50–60 Hz) treat vertical motion as an isolated 1-DOF vertical lift system—which is the exact loop you are designing in this lab.
